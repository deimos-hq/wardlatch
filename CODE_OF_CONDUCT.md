# Code of conduct

Wardlatch is maintained by one person. This is short because a one-maintainer project adopting a document about
committees and appeal processes it does not have would be pretending, and the point of a code of conduct is to tell
you what will actually happen.

## What is expected

Be straightforward and civil. Disagree about the work as much as you like: a sharp technical objection, especially
"this said SAFE and it was wrong", is often the most useful thing in a thread. Direct it at the code, the claim or the
decision, never at the person.

Not welcome here:

- Personal attacks, insults, or demeaning comments.
- Harassment of any kind, in the issue tracker or anywhere else this project reaches, including unwelcome attention
  after being asked to stop.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission. That includes pasting someone else's
  Wardlatch output, paths or keys into an issue.
- Sustained disruption of discussion.

## Where it applies

In this repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code review) and to anyone representing the
project in public.

## Reporting

Message Deimos AI LLC on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/deimoshq/). It reaches the maintainer, who
will move you to a private channel. You can also use GitHub's "Report content" on any comment.

Say what happened and roughly when. You do not need a formal case or to have been the target; if something looked
wrong, say so.

You will get an acknowledgement within **72 hours**. What follows depends on what happened: often a private word is
enough, sometimes a comment is removed, and in a serious or repeated case an account is blocked from the repository.
You will be told what was done.

Reports are kept private. Your identity is not shared with the person you reported without asking you first.

**A security vulnerability is not a conduct issue.** See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), so it reaches the right process.

## The obvious limitation

There is one maintainer, so a report about the maintainer has nowhere internal to go. If that is your situation,
GitHub's own reporting is the escalation path, and using it is a reasonable thing to do rather than a hostile one.
